# Yeruham Koplowitz: identity and parentage research

Latest research checkpoint: 21 October 2026. **Salomon's Pless/Mezerzitz household is identified;
his father's identity and parents remain unresolved.** Earlier dated checkpoints below are retained as history.

## Question and baseline

Can Yeruham, the reported father of Salomon Koplowitz, be identified in original records? Who were his
parents and spouse, and where did he live?

The starting evidence is the supplied **Outline Descendant Report for Yeruham Koplowitz**, page 1.
It gives Yeruham no dates or location and leaves his spouse unnamed. Its only listed child is Salomon
(1836–9 July 1903), married to Marie Gruenpeter (5 October 1848–17 May 1932). These are assertions
in a compiled family report, not independently verified vital records.

The unchanged starting tree is preserved in [commit 46fe665](https://github.com/avihil1/koplowitz-pasternak-family-tree/tree/46fe6655732473ac2261f85a0803d9282a243f4a).
The original [PDF](../Koplowitz_Pasternak.pdf) and [transcription](../transcription.txt) remain the baseline.
No new relative has been attached at this checkpoint. The absence of parents in this tree does not establish
that records naming them do not survive.

### Probable burial site — 21 October 2026

The indexed Jeruham was most likely buried in the **Old Jewish Cemetery, Bytom (Beuthen), Poland**, on present-day
Piastów Bytomskich Street (formerly Pstrowskiego). This is an inference from the cemetery category, 1862 date
and film1184405: [JOWBR cemetery POL-04049](https://www.jewishgen.org/databases/cemetery/jowbrshow.php?ID=POL-04049)
explicitly assigns the old cemetery register to that film. It is not a direct reading of Jeruham's original entry.

The [National Heritage Institute entry](https://zabytek.pl/en/obiekty/g-298876), with a description credited to
POLIN Museum, locates the old grounds on Piastów Bytomskich Street within the surrounding central-city block.
It states that above-ground features were removed in 1965, without exhuming the bodies; the surviving stones
were subsequently used for a memorial at the new Jewish cemetery. The old site is now a garden square with
a memorial plaque. The [new cemetery](https://sztetl.org.pl/pl/miejscowosci/b/419-bytom/114-cmentarze/9065-cmentarz-zydowski-w-bytomiu-ul-piekarska-56)
is at Piekarska56. A relocated stone would not establish that the person's remains were moved there.

Jeruham's exact grave location and any surviving stone remain unverified; reference727 is not a proven plot number.
